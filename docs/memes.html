Compressed models

Memes

Some ideas get clearer when compressed. One sentence, one contrast, or one question can expose a structure that was hiding in plain sight.

Look at the world. Then look at the human reproducing it.

Human mirror

What became normal inside the human?

Systems do not continue by themselves. Humans learn how to live inside them, then carry parts of them forward.

A human sleeps outside. Thousands walk past. When did this start feeling normal?
A child becomes quiet after fear or humiliation. Did the problem disappear, or did the child learn what could not safely move?
A machine removes work nobody needs to do anymore. Why does success become danger for the human whose labor disappeared?
Another group becomes “the enemy.” What changed inside the human that made their suffering easier to accept?
The world shapes the human.

Childhood, relationships, material conditions, authority, belonging, danger, culture, and repeated experience.

The human shapes the world.

Humans operate, defend, repeat, resist, repair, abandon, and replace what they inherit.

Change the structure without changing the human, and the old pattern can return. Change the human without changing the surrounding conditions, and the conditions can keep reproducing the pattern.
Look upstream

The visible outcome is often late in the story.

What we call “the problem” may be the point where a much longer process finally became visible.

Visible A human is living on the street.
←
Look earlier

Safety. Belonging. Care. Health. Money. Relationships. Protection. Development. Housing. Connection.

Visible A human harms another human.
←
Look earlier

What shaped the human? What pressures were active? What danger exists now? What must change so the harm is not reproduced?

Visible A child stops telling the truth.
←
Look earlier

What happened when truth previously created fear, shame, punishment, rejection, or conflict?

Visible A human cannot get needed care.
←
Look earlier

Who has the knowledge? Who has the tools? What blocks access? Why was that barrier built?

A useful test

Named ≠ explained.

Naming the output can help us talk about it. It can also make us stop looking.

Crime Named.

What produced the behavior?

Unemployment Named.

War Named.

What keeps rebuilding the conditions for war?

Hold both

We say Automation is progress.
And yet Successful automation can threaten the human whose work disappeared.
We say We want less harm.
And yet Much of our response begins after the harm.
